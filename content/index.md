Writing by [Christopher Harper](https://christopherjharper.com?utm_source=garden&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=garden&utm_content=homepage-intro) about operational leadership, safety, and how organizations learn from difficult work.

Most of these ideas began during the decade I spent in EMS as an EMT, field training officer, and operations supervisor. They have since followed me into incident investigation, systems thinking, software, and the company I am building.

This is the public part of my working notebook: selected and edited so another person can follow the thought, while keeping the links, revisions, and uncertainty that show how it developed. [[about-these-notes|About These Notes]] explains that boundary.

## Selected writing

### From records to learning

- [[what-the-form-cannot-see|What the Form Can't See]] — What incident forms capture, what remains in narrative, and what the record cannot establish.
- [[incident-reports-can-be-accurate-and-still-incomplete|The Report Was Accurate. It Was Still Incomplete.]] — A faithful record of an event can still leave a safety team without the context needed to learn from it.
- [[build-the-sprinkler-system|Build the Sprinkler System]] — Competent recovery can hide the system work that would make recovery less necessary.
- [[fleet-safety|Fleet Safety]] — A measured accident-reduction project where several of these ideas became practical.

### Leadership without dependence

- [[stop-being-the-answer|Stop Being the Answer]] — Expertise can make a supervisor useful while quietly making the team dependent.
- [[the-debrief|The Debrief]] — A repeatable way to help people recover and strengthen their own judgment after difficult work.
- [[recognition-is-evidence-of-attention|Recognition Is Evidence of Attention]] — Useful recognition makes otherwise invisible work legible.
- [[hard-conversations|Hard Conversations]] — Clarity without humiliation, delay, or avoidance.

### Learning the work

- [[first-90-days|First 90 Days as an EMS Supervisor]] — What to pay attention to before trying to fix everything.
- [[training-ftos|Training FTOs]] — Teaching experienced people how to develop somebody else.
- [[operational-metrics|Operational Metrics]] — Reading measures together instead of managing one number at a time.
- [[a-problem-passed-around-is-owned-by-nobody|A Problem Passed Around Is a Problem Owned by Nobody]] — Why handoffs can create motion without resolution.

## Follow the connections

The essays are entrances, not a reading order. Links inside them trace ideas across leadership, operations, safety, and product design. The graph beside each note shows nearby connections and backlinks show where an idea reappears.

Use the [[garden-map|Garden Map]] for the larger shape. [[open-questions|Open Questions]] holds the parts I have not resolved. The status and last-tended date on each page show how settled a note is and when I last changed it.

One branch of this thinking became [ChatIR](https://chatir.io?utm_source=garden&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=garden&utm_content=homepage), software for helping operational and safety teams recover context from incident records and see recurring conditions without handing judgment to the software. The [[chatir|ChatIR note]] follows the ideas, boundaries, and unanswered questions behind it.
