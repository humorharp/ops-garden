# Open Questions

These are not a backlog I expect to close. They are the questions that keep showing up underneath otherwise different work.

I am publishing them because a body of thought made only from conclusions is not an honest record of thinking.

## Where does empowerment end and abandonment begin?

I believe supervisors should [[stop-being-the-answer|stop making themselves the answer to every question]]. I also know that “use your judgment” can be a cowardly thing to tell someone when leadership has not given them information, authority, or protection.

How do you move a decision carefully enough that the person gains judgment rather than merely inheriting risk? How do you know when a question is permission-seeking and when it is really a request for a leader to share the burden?

## How much context should a formal record carry?

[[incident-reports-can-be-accurate-and-still-incomplete|An incident report can be accurate and still omit conditions that matter for learning]]. But there are good reasons a patient-care report, employee report, or compliance record does not contain every operational detail.

The answer cannot be “put everything in the form.” That produces worse forms and often worse information.

What belongs in the record of the event? What belongs in the later investigation? How do you preserve the connection between them without confusing their purposes?

## When does helpful adaptation become hidden system debt?

People doing essential work adapt constantly. Most of the time that is competence. Sometimes the adaptation allows a broken system to appear healthy because the frontline quietly absorbs the cost.

[[build-the-sprinkler-system|Building the sprinkler system]] requires making those quiet saves visible without punishing the people who made them. I do not think most organizations know how to do that consistently. I am not sure I do either.

## What should AI be allowed to conclude from an operational record?

The line I currently use for [[chatir|chatIR]] is: equip the decision; do not make it.

That sounds clean. The implementation is not.

An analysis can show recurrence, co-occurrence, missing information, or a candidate relationship. None of that automatically establishes cause. A suggested control can be reasonable and still be wrong for the local operation. How should software express uncertainty without becoming useless? How should it help a safety team focus attention without laundering a statistical pattern into an authoritative conclusion?

## How do you preserve a real story without using another person as material?

Most of what I know came from work involving actual people on difficult days. The examples matter because they keep the ideas attached to reality. Privacy and dignity matter more.

I can remove names and still leave someone recognizable. I can combine examples and accidentally invent a cleaner story than any real event. I can abstract so aggressively that the lesson becomes weightless.

The current rule is to publish the pattern first and the story only when the story adds something the pattern cannot—and only after it can be told without exposing someone who never asked to become part of my body of work.

## Can a public Garden remain honest after it becomes useful commercially?

This site is connected to my work. It would be silly to pretend otherwise. [[chatir|chatIR]] grew from many of the ideas here, and the [[tools|Field Tools]] package some of them for use.

But if every note bends toward a call to action, the Garden stops being a thinking environment and becomes content marketing with backlinks.

I want the work to create trust because it is useful and honest, not because every path has been engineered toward a conversion. Holding that boundary will probably require ongoing attention.
