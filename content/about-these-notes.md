# About These Notes

I have a lot of notes.

That is probably an understatement. I started carrying paper notebooks because I wanted somewhere to put random thoughts. Then OneNote was going to organize my life. Then Roam was going to connect everything. Then Obsidian was going to let me own it all. Each tool helped. None of them relieved me of the responsibility of actually deciding what anything meant.

This site is one attempt to do that.

These are not my raw notes. My private Obsidian vaults contain meeting notes, journals, unfinished ideas, duplicated ideas, bad ideas, work records, family details, names that do not belong on the internet, and thoughts written while I was still figuring out what I thought. I am not publishing that archive.

The Garden is the part I choose to make public.

I want it polished enough that another person can follow the thought, but not polished until all the fingerprints disappear. If I changed my mind, I want the earlier belief to remain visible where it helps. If an idea came from something I saw in the field, I want to preserve that origin without turning another person's difficult day into my content. If I am uncertain, I would rather say so than make the prose sound confident for no reason.

## This is a wiki, not a blog

Most of these pages are not meant to stand alone. They are parts of a larger argument about operational work, supervision, safety, learning, and the systems we build around people doing hard things.

You can read a page from top to bottom. You can also follow a link halfway through and never come back. That is probably closer to how the ideas developed anyway.

Some notes are practical, like [[first-90-days|what to focus on during your first 90 days as a supervisor]]. Some are working claims, like [[recognition-is-evidence-of-attention|recognition is evidence of attention]]. Some are the questions underneath the company I am building, like [[incident-reports-can-be-accurate-and-still-incomplete|how an accurate incident report can still be operationally incomplete]].

The status of a note tells you how I currently think about it:

- **Reference** notes are maps, tools, or explanations meant to be fairly stable.
- **Evergreen** notes are durable ideas I expect to keep refining.
- **Growing** notes have enough shape to share but still contain real unresolved edges.
- **Seedling** notes are early. Most seedlings stay private until there is enough there to be useful.

Nothing here is finished merely because it has a URL.

These are living notes. I will revise them, link them differently, add context, and sometimes change my mind. If you come back and a sentence has moved—or a conclusion has softened—do not be alarmed. You are not misremembering it. The note changed because I did.

## Why make it public?

The immediate reason is usefulness. I spent about a decade in EMS as an EMT, field training officer, and operations supervisor. I learned a lot through calls, mistakes, investigations, debriefs, projects, and conversations that I wish somebody had explained to me earlier. If you are doing that work now, I hope some of it saves you time or helps you see a problem differently.

There is another reason that feels slightly more embarrassing to say out loud.

I am building [[chatir|chatIR]], and I want a record of the thinking that produced it. Not a founder story reconstructed afterward, where every old event magically points toward the company and every detour becomes part of the plan. The actual path was messier. The ideas came from fleet safety, training, documentation, investigations, difficult calls, leadership mistakes, software experiments, and years of writing things down before I knew they were related.

Maybe nobody will care about that path later. That is entirely possible. But if someone does—if they want to understand how I thought while building this—I would rather leave them the real trail than a polished mythology.

And even if that future reader never arrives, making the trail legible is useful to me now.

## The boundary matters

This Garden is curated by me. An idea appearing in my private notes does not entitle it to publication.

I remove identifying details unless they are already intentionally public and necessary. I do not publish patient information. I do not name employees, customers, or organizations merely to make an example feel concrete. I do not turn uncertain memories into clean case studies. When a measurement matters, I try to preserve what it actually measured.

Cleaned up does not mean invented. Anonymous does not mean context-free. Professional does not have to mean bloodless.

That is the balance I am trying to hold.
